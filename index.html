1. Select Matrix Phase (Base)
Polymer (Epoxy)
Low cost, lightweight, easy to process. Limited temperature resistance (<200°C).
Metal (Aluminum/Ti)
Higher temp tolerance, ductile, good shear strength. Moderately heavy.
Ceramic (Silicon Carbide)
Extreme thermal tolerance (>1000°C), chemically inert. Highly brittle matrix.
2. Select Reinforcement Fiber
Carbon Fiber
Extremely high tensile strength and stiffness. Excellent strength-to-weight ratio.
Glass Fiber
Inexpensive, flexible, non-conductive. Heavier than carbon with moderate stiffness.
Kevlar (Aramid)
Outstanding impact energy absorption & fracture toughness. Poor compression strength.
Silicon Carbide (SiC)
High strength under extreme thermal environments. Very expensive and hard to manufacture.
3. Select Fiber Architecture
Continuous Unidirectional
Maximum strength along a single principal loading axis. Weak transversely.
Continuous Woven Fabric
Balanced bi-directional strength (2D plane). Resist delamination & impact better.
Short Random Discontinuous
Equal properties in all directions (isotropic). Enables complex 3D shape molding.
